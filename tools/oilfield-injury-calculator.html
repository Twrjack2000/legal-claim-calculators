Third-Party Oilfield Lawsuits vs. Workers' Compensation

When a roughneck, derrickhand, roustabout, wireline operator, or plant technician is injured on a multi-employer drill site or refinery, employers often tell the worker that Workers' Compensation is their sole remedy. While Workers' Comp bars most direct lawsuits against your own W-2 payroll employer, it does not protect third-party companies working on the same pad or facility.

If an employee of the well operator (Company Man), directional drilling firm, frac crew, crane contractor, mud company, or trucking company caused the hazard—or if a piece of rig hardware failed—you can file an uncapped Third-Party Personal Injury Lawsuit alongside your Workers' Comp claim. Third-party tort claims recover 100% of lost day-rate and overtime earnings, full future earning capacity, physical pain, mental anguish, and disfigurement damages.

Citations under OSHA (29 CFR 1910 / 1926) for Process Safety Management (PSM) failures, Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) violations, H2S gas monitoring failures, or blowout preventer (BOP) defects serve as powerful evidence of corporate negligence. Wilful or Repeat OSHA violations frequently double or triple settlement valuations.
If you work on a navigable vessel, semi-submersible, drillship, or jack-up rig, you may qualify as a Jones Act Seaman under 46 U.S.C. § 30104. Unlike state workers' comp, the Jones Act grants you the right to sue your direct employer for negligence and Vessel Unseaworthiness, in addition to Maintenance and Cure.
