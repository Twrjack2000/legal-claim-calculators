Understanding Uber & Lyft's 3-Period Insurance System

Unlike standard car accidents where recovery is often bottlenecked by a driver's minimum personal auto policy, crashes involving Transportation Network Companies (TNCs) like Uber and Lyft are governed by a tiered commercial insurance framework:

Period 1 (App On, Waiting for Match): If the driver is logged into the driver app waiting for a ping, personal auto insurance typically denies coverage due to the "commercial livery exclusion." Uber and Lyft provide contingent liability limits of $50,000 per injured person / $100,000 total per accident plus $25,000 property damage.

Period 2 (Match Accepted, En Route) & Period 3 (Passenger in Car): The instant the driver taps "Accept" through the moment the passenger exits the vehicle, a $1,000,000 primary commercial liability policy activates. Paying passengers in Period 3 hold the strongest legal leverage in personal injury law because they bear 0% comparative fault regardless of which driver caused the wreck.

Frequently Asked Questions

Rideshare insurance operates in three distinct app phases: Period 1 (App ON, waiting for a ride request) provides $50,000 per person / $100,000 per accident in contingent liability coverage. Period 2 (Ride accepted, driver en route to pick up passenger) and Period 3 (Passenger inside the vehicle until drop-off) unlock Uber and Lyft's full $1,000,000 commercial third-party liability policy.
As a paying rideshare passenger in Period 3, you have zero comparative fault. If your Uber/Lyft driver was at fault, the $1,000,000 commercial liability policy applies. If another motorist caused the crash and is uninsured or carries state-minimum limits, Uber and Lyft carry high-limit Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM) coverage in most states to cover your damages.
While Uber and Lyft classify drivers as 1099 independent contractors to avoid direct vicarious employment liability, state Transportation Network Company (TNC) statutes mandate that their $1,000,000 primary commercial insurance policies (administered by insurers like Progressive, Farmers, Allstate, or Liberty Mutual) cover bodily injuries caused during active rides.
Because Period 2 and Period 3 crashes are backed by a $1,000,000 commercial policy rather than a $15,000–$30,000 personal policy, rideshare settlements average significantly more than standard car wrecks. Soft-tissue claims with physical therapy often settle for $20,000–$45,000, disc herniations with epidural injections range from $75,000–$225,000, and surgical injuries regularly settle between $250,000 and $1,000,000.
