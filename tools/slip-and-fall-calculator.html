How Insurance Adjusters Value Slip-and-Fall & Premises Claims

Premises liability claims are among the most fiercely defended cases in tort law. Because there is no police traffic citation assigning automatic fault, commercial insurers like Sedgwick, Travelers, and Liberty Mutual evaluate three primary pillars: Notice, Severity (Surgical vs. Non-Surgical), and Comparative Fault.

1. Constructive Notice & Sweep Logs: When an attorney subpoenas a supermarket or hotel's hourly inspection sweep logs and CCTV camera footage showing employees walking past a liquid spill, ice patch, or torn carpet without remedying it, the defense loses its ability to claim the hazard "just happened." Even stronger are Building Code Violations (IBC/NFPA life-safety violations for stair riser height variance, lack of handrails, or inadequate illumination), which establish negligence per se.

2. Surgical vs. Non-Surgical Multipliers: Falls that cause objective fractures (Colles wrist fracture, trimalleolar ankle fracture, femoral neck hip fracture) or require arthroscopic/spinal surgery command multipliers 2× to 3× higher than soft-tissue sprains because defense doctors cannot credibly label a broken bone "pre-existing degeneration."

Frequently Asked Questions

Slip-and-fall settlements depend heavily on whether surgery was required and whether the property is commercial. Non-surgical sprains or soft-tissue falls typically settle between $15,000 and $45,000. Fractured wrists, ankles, hips, or herniated discs requiring injections often settle between $60,000 and $175,000. Falls requiring orthopedic hardware surgery, hip replacement, or spinal fusion at major commercial properties regularly settle for $200,000 to $750,000+.
Simply falling on someone's property does not automatically make them liable. To win a premises liability claim, you must prove the property owner or store either (1) created the hazard, (2) had Actual Notice (knew about the spill/defect and failed to fix or warn), or (3) had Constructive Notice (the hazard existed long enough—proven by footprints, dirty liquid, or CCTV sweep logs—that a reasonable inspection would have discovered it).
Insurance defense attorneys routinely argue that the hazard was 'open and obvious' and that you were looking at your phone or not watching your step. This defense is used to assign 20%–40% comparative fault to the victim, directly reducing your net payout. Building code violations (like uneven stair risers, missing handrails, or poor lighting) defeat the open-and-obvious defense in most jurisdictions.
Most commercial general liability (CGL) policies carry $5,000 to $10,000 in no-fault Medical Payments ('MedPay') coverage that pays initial ER bills regardless of fault—separate from your bodily injury liability claim.
